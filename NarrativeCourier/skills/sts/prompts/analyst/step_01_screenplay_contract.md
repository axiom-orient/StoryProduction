# STEP 1 — Screenplay Contract & Adaptation Budget

Using the Human-approved Source IR, define the conversion contract rather than a formula.

Include:
- source identity statement and protected obligations;
- intended audience experience and dramatic question;
- POV / audience-information policy;
- allowable adaptation space and Human-approved structural inventions;
- genre promises actually supported by source;
- target length/production constraints from config;
- `Adaptation Pressure: LOW|MODERATE|HIGH|EXTREME`;
- an explicit adaptation budget: which obligations can be `DRAMATIZE`, where minimal `CONNECT` detail is needed, which approved GAPs may be `EXPAND`, and which proposed `INVENT` choices require Human authority;
- source-form policy: which form functions must survive and what may change;
- semantic-coda/ending policy: what final meaning must be recoverable without requiring literal prose;
- explicit non-goals and prohibited drift.

A short source does not imply a short target. If target duration greatly exceeds supported source material, report expansion pressure instead of silently manufacturing subplot volume.

Choose no structural template unless the material benefits from it; justify any chosen form from source/audience requirements.
