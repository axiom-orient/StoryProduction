# Adaptation Pressure

STS converts a source into a target-length screenplay. Source length alone never selects the workflow: a short source may become a short film or a feature.

## Pressure model

Judge pressure from three independent inputs:

1. **Target scale** — requested screen duration and production constraints.
2. **Source coverage** — how much causal, behavioral and spatial material the approved Source IR actually contains.
3. **Semantic risk** — protected relations, world rules, knowledge boundaries, ending sensitivity and unresolved GAP/CON items.

Classify `LOW | MODERATE | HIGH | EXTREME`.

High pressure means more of the target cannot be obtained by dramatizing existing source obligations. It does **not** authorize invention.

## Expansion discipline

Every material connective or expansion decision must be one of:

- `DRAMATIZE` — make an implicit source obligation observable;
- `CONNECT` — add the smallest causal/temporal bridge needed between preserved obligations;
- `EXPAND` — develop an approved GAP without changing protected meaning;
- `INVENT` — add a new structural obligation; requires explicit Human authority.

When pressure is `HIGH` or `EXTREME`, list the unresolved expansion budget before structural writing. Never hide expansion inside scene prose.

## Compression discipline

When the target is shorter than the source, prefer `COMBINE` and `OMIT` only when protected assertions, relations, agency, epistemic status, ending outcome and ending meaning remain recoverable.

## Non-goal

Do not optimize for a conventional page count or beat count. Pressure is an authority/risk signal, not a formula score.
