# Color, Light, and Material

## Color script
Use project-level color beats to make light/color carry story progression. A beat may move from warm/open/high-key to restricted/cold/low-key while the render language remains stable. Do not force every scene to the same palette if the narrative needs contrast.

## Motivated lighting
Describe a believable source or deliberate graphic source:
- window daylight,
- practical lamp,
- overcast sky,
- fire,
- screen glow,
- theatrical top light,
- stylized rim motivated by the scene language.

Then specify direction, source size/softness, contrast, and color relationship. Avoid “dramatic/cinematic lighting” as the only instruction.

## Character and emotion first
Lighting/framing should serve the character and scene beat. If a background fact does not help the story, suppress it instead of making every surface equally impressive.

## Material truth
Materials need physical cues, not adjectives:
- worn linen: soft weave, frayed edge, low specular response,
- brushed metal: directional micro-scratches, controlled highlight rolloff,
- wet stone: darkened base value, localized reflections,
- watercolor paper: pigment granulation and visible paper tooth.

For stylized projects, preserve the chosen abstraction; “material truth” means consistent rules, not forced photorealism.
