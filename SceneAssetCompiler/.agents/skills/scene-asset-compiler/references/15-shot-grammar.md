# Shot Grammar

A generated scene should be specified as a shot, not a bag of nouns.

## Narrative function
Pick the reason for the frame: establish, reveal, intimacy, isolation, threat, action, transition, evidence/detail, or iconic key art. The label is not decorative; it determines what must read first.

## Emotional intent
State the intended viewer/character feeling separately from style. Translate it into framing, distance, light, value, color, atmosphere, and blocking rather than leaving it as a vague mood adjective.

## First-read hierarchy
State 1–3 reads in order. Example:
1. daughter discovering the trail,
2. long shadows of the objects forming a path,
3. dangerous mountain route at dusk.

## Composition
Specify only decisions that matter:
- framing / shot size,
- camera angle,
- perspective / lens behavior / focus-depth language,
- subject placement,
- foreground / midground / background layers,
- negative space when needed for isolation, motion, UI, or copy.

Do not force a lens number for stylized art unless it conveys a real perspective requirement.

## Blocking
Describe relationships spatially: left/right, behind/in front, distance, occlusion, gaze/action direction. For multiple characters, state who occupies which depth plane and who looks at whom.

## Readability test
Review at thumbnail size and full size. If the intended first read disappears at thumbnail scale, reduce competing contrast/detail before adding more detail.
